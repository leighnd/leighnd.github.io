## Current projects:
We have a diverse array of exciting projects ongoing in the lab (see below). 


1. Delineating the role of adaptive immunity in complex tissue regeneration. We have recently generated the first immunodeficient newts and shown that adaptive immunity is dispensable for complex tissue regeneration (see Publications). During this work, we uncovered that the regenerating niche has immunosuppressive function. We are now focused on understanding immunosuppressive mechanisms present in newts. This involves teasing apart mechanisms of central and peripheral tolerance and if and how these impact regenerative capacity. 

2. Exploring cancer resistance mechanisms in newts. Newts have famously high cancer resistance (see [seminal work by Charles Breedis](https://aacrjournals.org/cancerres/article/12/12/861/472716/Induction-of-Accessory-Limbs-and-of-Sarcoma-in-the)). This fascinating feature has not been thoroughly investigated from the molecular perspective. We are now exploring how regenerative capacity may endow tumor resistance. Generating newts that lack tumor suppressor genes and genotoxic challenges are allowing us to better understand the biological underpinnings of tumor resistance. As an added bonus, we are interested in comparing how the immune response to tumors converges or diverges from that of regenerating tissue. 

3. Genomics of regeneration. In a fruitful collaboration we have recently assembled [the genome](https://www.cell.com/cell-genomics/fulltext/S2666-979X(25)00017-5) of the newt em>Pleurodeles waltl</em>. This is enabling an entirely new approach to study salamanders. We are now delving into the genomics of limb regeneration, assessing how gene expression is regulated prior to and throughout regeneration. We are also interested in how transposable elements contribute to newts regenerative capacity. 

We have other projects in the works and are always happy to hear from people interested in joining the lab and for them to pitch any ideas that would be interested to explore using the salamander model system!
